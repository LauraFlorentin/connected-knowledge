# Review, repair, and optional learning

## Review through actual use

Choose a meaningful task: recover a decision rationale, explain a concept, compare positions, or assemble an argument. Inspect a bounded relevant sample, including a source, an idea, a map, and a note used in a project where available. State the sample and limitations; do not generalize it into a whole-vault verdict.

Inspect purpose, standalone meaning, scope, provenance, distinctness, defensible relationships, retrieval, and reuse. For every important finding, give a concrete example, consequence, and smallest effective repair. Prioritize unsupported or inaccurate claims before cosmetic inconsistencies. Note count and graph density do not establish quality; do not produce a composite maturity score.

## Repair proportionately

- Missing source: restore the evidence boundary; mark unverified claims rather than inventing attribution.
- Unclear or overbroad claim: name referents and retain conditions or limits.
- Similar notes: compare scope and evidence before proposing consolidation.
- Weak link: explain its function or present it as topical navigation rather than support.
- Retrieval failure: try a useful alias, clearer title, or map link on the affected sample.
- Large unused archive: develop a small subset for a current question; do not assume archival storage itself is a failure.
- Review backlog: prioritize by actual use, consequence, and sensitivity to changing evidence rather than age alone.

Respect the task's edit scope. For review-only requests, give proposed repairs. For requested fixes, read targets first, preserve history and user prose, verify changes, and inspect affected links for renames. Keep a reversible mapping for substantial renames. Do not delete notes or change app configuration as a side effect. Flag downstream effects outside scope. A historical source note need not agree with newer knowledge, and an unresolved question can remain useful.

Choose one reversible process experiment when useful: describe the observed bottleneck, plausible cause, small change, real task to try, and evidence for retaining or undoing it. Measure whether a note was found, a source checked, or a draft supported. Avoid compulsory routines or additional metadata dashboards.

## Optional learning

For an explicit learning or coaching request, offer one relevant exercise: explain a mechanism through a new example, apply it in another context, identify a counterexample, or distinguish it from a neighboring concept. Use the response to improve the note. Do not require exercises before delivering capture, import, or writing work. Do not equate assistant-generated prose with demonstrated user comprehension. Fit requested routines to available time without imposing streaks or gamification.

## Deliver

Report inspected scope, what works, important findings with examples, completed repairs versus proposals, and one next experiment when useful. Use existing `review_status` only after actual editorial review; it does not certify factual truth. Do not introduce parallel `status` or `type` properties. Create a review record only when useful or requested; if saved under this ontology, use an artifact with `artifact_type: report`.
